- **Rook's Rampage / "Rampaging Rook"** (chessworld.net, scribd lesson doc): rook + several enemy pawns/erasers on board; rook wins by capturing all pawns before any reach far side. Full 8x8 board implied, not reduced.
- **Rook vs Pawns** (littlechesschamps.com/mini-games): pawns win by reaching end or capturing rook; rook wins by capturing all pawns. Full board.

- **Acorn Chess "Pawns vs pieces"** (acornchess.com/minigames, VERIFIED via WebFetch): several pawns vs one rook/bishop/queen/knight; lone piece wins by capturing all or blocking last pawn. No board-size or age given on the page.

## Q4. "Ai ăn hết trước" (rook+bishop each side, capture all)

Two directly relevant documented games, both VERIFIED:
1. **"Good Trade, Bad Trade"** (northwestchess.info/teachchess.html, via WebSearch synthesis — direct WebFetch failed, site's TLS cert expired): all 8 bishops+rooks on starting squares, rooks shifted sideways so they don't pre-attack; White first; goal is teaching trade value (rook > bishop), not "capture all" as primary win condition — it's a trade-recognition drill, questions like "can you attack my rook?" Also lists follow-up "Queen vs Four Pieces" (queen vs all enemy bishops+rooks).
2. **"Bishops + Rooks"** (chess.com blog by capatalfish3, VERIFIED via WebFetch): white moves bishop first, black moves rook first, **first capture wins** (not capture-all). Author states "draw with best play," reaching that skill "may take a second grader a month of lessons." Explicitly recommended as the *entry-point* minigame after pawns-only.

**"Capture all pieces" as an explicit win condition** is documented generically (Exeter/Acorn/Little Chess Champs: "Capture All the Pieces" listed as a named category, VERIFIED) but not specifically as a "rook+bishop each side, capture everything" ruleset.

**Known failure modes with beginners**: not documented directly for this exact game. Adjacent documented caution: chess.com blog notes the harder follow-on "king opposition" minigame is "too abstract and tedious" for young kids unless tournament-track (VERIFIED) — implies escalating complexity too fast is a known failure mode in general, not specific to rook+bishop capture-all.

## Q5. Board orientation mnemonics + error rate
